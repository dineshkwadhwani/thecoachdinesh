Coaching Philosophy

Leadership is not a destination! It is a practice. And like any practice, it improves with deliberate reflection, honest feedback, and sustained commitment.

Over 25 years working inside leadership, not observing it from the outside, I have probably sat in every chair at the table. I have been the junior engineer trying to find my voice, the first-time manager navigating the shift from doing to leading, the senior director building a 400-person global organisation across four continents, and the executive accountable for hundreds of millions in revenue. I have led through rapid growth, through AI transformation, through cultural clashes between engineering teams in Israel, the UK, the US, and India, and through the organisational turbulence that comes with acquisition, restructuring, and change.

That breadth of lived experience is what I bring to coaching. I have worked with leaders at every level — from graduate engineers finding their first leadership voice, to directors navigating their first P&L, to Senior VPs shaping organisational strategy. I understand what each transition feels like from the inside, because I have made most of them myself.

The leaders I work with are self-aware, introspective, and committed to growth. They are willing to step back from the daily grind, examine the gap between who they are and who they want to become, and do the hard work of changing. My role is not to give them answers — it is to ask better questions, hold up a mirror, and hold them accountable to the change they say they want to make.
